Sign in for the first time

One email, one link, one password — you're in.

Your AHN or SHOPLINE contact set your migration up and sent you an email titled something like "You've been invited to Mercantor." It contains a one-time link.

  1. Open the email and click Set your password. The link is single-use and expires after a few days — if it has already been used or you can't find the email, see Getting help below for how to request a new one.
  2. Choose a password of at least 12 characters. There's no need to mix in symbols or numbers if you'd rather use a long, memorable phrase — length is what matters most.
  3. You're signed in immediately after — no separate confirmation step.

Providing access

The credentials AHN needs to actually move your store.

Things like your current platform's admin login, DNS access, or a payment gateway key. Anything marked needed to start is blocking AHN from beginning work, so those are worth doing first.

mercantor.co/portal/access

Store admin login Shopify or your current platform's back office. Requested Attach link Upload file
DNS access Verified by AHN on Sep 4. Verified

For each item, you can:

  • Attach a link — to a shared doc, a password manager entry, or wherever the credential already lives.
  • Upload a file — if it's easier to send as a document or screenshot.
  • Mark it Received or flag an Issue — if you sent it another way (email, a call) or something about it isn't working.
Note: only AHN can mark an item Verified. That's deliberate — it means someone has actually confirmed the credential works, not just that it was sent.

Getting help

Two people, always named, always one click away.

You are never routed through a general support queue. Your project has two named people responsible for it, both listed on your Overview page under Who to ask.

I lost my invite email, or the link expired

Go to mercantor.co/forgot-password and enter the email your migration was set up with. If an account exists for it, a fresh reset link arrives within a few minutes — check spam if it doesn't.

Can more than one person at my company get access?

Yes — ask your AHN project manager or SHOPLINE account manager to invite a teammate. Each person gets their own login scoped to the same project.

Will you ever ask for my password over email or phone?

No. AHN and SHOPLINE will never ask you to share your Mercantor password. The only legitimate password links come from mercantor.co itself, sent when you request them.

What if something on the portal looks wrong?

Leave a note in Activity or message your project manager directly — whichever is faster for you.

Bring a merchant on

One form creates the merchant record, the project, and every checklist.

Go to /projects/new. AHN Admin, AHN Project Manager, and every SHOPLINE role can do this — AHN Developer cannot, by design.

Submitting it does five things at once:

  1. Creates the Merchant record and its primary contact.
  2. Creates the Project with a code like PRJ-0042, starting in stage Introduction.
  3. Seeds the Access, Asset, and Scope checklists from your organization's standard templates — nothing starts from a blank page.
  4. Creates all five approval steps (Design, Development, QA, Merchant Final, SHOPLINE Deployment) as Not requested.
  5. Notifies whoever you assigned as AHN PM, AHN developer, or SHOPLINE AM/SE.
Searching first matters. The form looks up existing merchants as you type, scoped to your organization — use it before creating a duplicate record for a merchant you're already migrating a second store for.
3

Invite people

Two different invite flows — your colleagues, and the merchant.

Staff (AHN or SHOPLINE) get an org-wide account from /people. A merchant gets an account scoped to exactly one project, from inside that project.

AHN & SHOPLINE colleagues /people · requires "Manage people" Invite
The merchant project → Settings tab · requires "Manage merchant" Invite merchant

Either way it's the same underlying pattern: a real user record is created immediately with an unusable password, a one-time /set-password link is emailed out, and the account only becomes usable once that link is opened. Re-inviting an email that already has an account restores it rather than duplicating it.

Seventeen stages, four phases, one owner at a time.

Every project moves through the same ordered stages. At any moment exactly one team owns the next move — the Overview tab always names it.

Design, Development, and QA are decided by AHN (any delivery role). Merchant Final is the merchant's own call — though an AHN Admin or PM can record a decision that arrived by phone or email on their behalf, kept separately from who actually decided it.

Once Design, Development, QA and Merchant Final are all approved and the in-scope data is verified, the SHOPLINE handoff tab shows a checklist and a Submit for review button becomes available (AHN Admin/PM). SHOPLINE then decides: approve it — which opens the final SHOPLINE Deployment approval — or send it back with what needs fixing.

Nothing here is a status update someone forgot to send. Every decision is a row with a name and a timestamp — that record is what the Audit log and the Timeline tab both read from.

3

Start or refer a migration

SHOPLINE roles can create a project too, not just AHN.

When a merchant on your side is ready to move, use /projects/new — the same form AHN uses. It creates the merchant record, the project, and every checklist in one step, and notifies whoever you assign as the AHN project manager and developer.

Search for the merchant by name first — if they already have a project (a second store, say), the form finds it instead of creating a duplicate record.
4

Own the introduction

The first stage is yours by design — AHN can't start without it.

Every project opens in stage Introduction, owned by SHOPLINE. That's the send-the-introduction-email step — getting the merchant looped in and expecting AHN's outreach. From there the merchant moves it into Merchant Contacted.

Once the build is underway, the Activity tab on the project is where you stay in the loop — comment there rather than a side email thread, so AHN and the merchant see the same conversation you do.

AHN submits the finished build on the SHOPLINE handoff tab once their own checklist is fully met (scope verified, Design/Development/QA/Merchant Final all approved) — you can't be handed something half-done. From there:

  1. Approve the handoff, or send it back with what needs fixing — either way it's a real decision recorded with your name.
  2. Once approved, decide the final SHOPLINE Deployment approval — the moment the store actually goes live is yours to clear, not AHN's.
This is the one approval type only SHOPLINE roles can decide — not even an AHN Admin can grant it on your behalf.
